# Pulse-ox subsystem — review, changes, and what is left

A design review of the badge's MAX3010x heart-rate / SpO2 path, the changes it produced, and the work it
identified but did not do. Written so the reasoning can be checked rather than taken on trust: every
significant claim below was either verified in source, executed against a replay of the production
decision path, or measured on a real badge.

- [hardware-notes.md](hardware-notes.md) — findings from the KiCad sources, including BOM defects that
  matter before boards are built
- [bench-runbook.md](bench-runbook.md) — measurements to run on a badge, with interpretations registered
  in advance
- [calibration-protocol.md](calibration-protocol.md) — what each tier of validation would license you to
  claim

---

## The three that mattered most

**The badge silently refused to measure a large fraction of people.** The active-mode power-down gate used
an absolute DC threshold roughly 30× stricter than the finger-detection threshold that had just decided a
finger was present — and the grace period before it armed (3000 ms) was shorter than the time needed to
fill the first analysis window (4000 ms). So the first evaluation of every session was already eligible to
end it. Sweeping DC across the replay put the dead band at **IR DC 4000–70000 counts**: finger detected,
session killed, heart rate never shown. Above 70000 the same code worked 85% of the time. It worked for
whoever tuned it and failed everyone with weaker optical coupling.

**The stability gate could not withhold a number.** When SpO2 was in range but failed the stability test,
the code still latched the value *and refreshed the hold timestamp*, so the hold was permanently valid.
Stability only ever chose between showing the median and showing the raw value — never between showing a
number and showing none. Captured on real hardware: a finger sliding off the sensor produced
**"SpO2 98%, stable"** three evaluations running while the heart-rate estimator behind it swung between 33
and 214 bpm. The heart-rate path correctly refused all of it; the SpO2 path did not.

**A dead red channel reported a healthy number.** The vendor lookup table is non-monotonic — it peaks at
100 across a wide plateau and returns 95–97 as the ratio approaches zero. So a red channel degraded to
*nothing* reports **97%, flagged stable**, on 44 of 44 evaluations. Degrading the channel makes the reading
look better. No downstream logic can recover from that, which is why red-channel integrity is now an
explicit gate.

## Also fixed

- **Die temperature was transmitted as body temperature.** It went out over LoRa and MQTT in a protobuf
  field documented as "Body temperature in degrees Celsius", where third-party clients render it as
  exactly that — the one defect here that escaped the badge entirely. It is now shown only locally and
  explicitly labelled `die:`.
- **Biometric broadcast was on by default**, and enabling the health *screen* silently enabled *mesh
  transmission* too. Now opt-in, and the two are decoupled.
- **Noise was accepted as a finger.** Finger detection accepted an absolute AC threshold *or* pulsatility;
  since noise clears any absolute threshold, the pulsatility constant already in the header was bypassed.
  Both are now required, and periodicity is checked as well.
- **The presence-detection log had never shown real data.** A rate-limiter interaction meant it always
  fired on the one call per wake cycle where the sample buffer had just been cleared — 158 of 158 lines in
  a five-minute capture read `mean_ir=0 n=1`. The thresholds it exists to inform could never have been
  validated from it.
- **The LED rail was clocked before it came up.** `powerStrips()` waited 1 ms; the real ramp is 7–11 ms
  depending on a bulk capacitor whose value is unspecified in the hardware design.

## Method note

Findings were generated by independent expert critiques, then adversarially verified — which mattered:
of 114 initial findings, 40 were downgraded as overstated and 6 refuted outright. Several headline claims
did not survive contact with either a replay harness or the hardware:

- A predicted "30 seconds of phantom readings after finger removal" turned out to be **~3 seconds** in
  reality; the model assumed an instant DC collapse, whereas a real lift decays gradually and trips
  finger-detection promptly.
- Harmonic doubling of the heart rate was real but affected **1.4%** of the physiological parameter space,
  not the broad failure initially claimed.
- LED-to-photodiode crosstalk required an implausible optical leak (>2× the cardiac AC) before it captured
  the estimator.
- The "bare PCB with no enclosure" premise was simply wrong — there is a bolted two-part enclosure.
- A proposal to "rebalance the optics to move R" was mathematically impossible: the AC/DC ratio is
  invariant to LED drive current, so no register change moves R.

Where a number appears in this documentation it came from execution or measurement. Where something is
unresolved, the runbook says how to resolve it rather than guessing.

## Validated on hardware

The changes were flashed to a real badge and measured across ten conditions (~20 captures): steady, firm,
light and offset contact; cold-induced vasoconstriction; bright light; and no-finger baselines in dark,
room-lit, torch-lit and face-down-on-a-desk. On comparable signal quality, SpO2 availability on good
contact went from 42% to 93% while heart rate stayed at 100%, and deliberately poor contact is now
withheld rather than displayed.

Hardware also caught five regressions introduced during this work that native tests did not: a pulsatility
gate that rejected real fingers, two separate consequences of making broadcast opt-in (the sensor stopped
being serviced when the screen slept, and the service cadence dropped so far that presence detection could
never fire), an over-aggressive FIFO invalidation that stopped all measurement, and an HR threshold set
too tight. Three of those were thresholds fitted to too little data - the same failure mode as the
original 70000-count bar.

Three things worth knowing from the measurements:

- **The sensor is readout-noise limited, not photon limited.** Measured floor is ~4.0 counts rms and flat
  across a 16x change in LED drive - shot noise would scale as sqrt(I) and predicts 0.15. Against a
  326-count cardiac AC that is an SNR of 78x (38 dB). An earlier revision of these notes claimed 293x from
  shot noise alone; that was wrong by 3.7x, though the contact-limited conclusion it supported still holds.
- **Perfusion index is inversely correlated with signal quality on this badge.** Good contact measures
  0.208%, firm pressure 0.453%, a barely-resting finger 1.683%, because poor contact produces large
  aperiodic excursions that dwarf a pulse. Any amplitude-based gate here is directionally wrong.
- **Bright light does not block detection, it crushes the pulse.** Ambient-light cancellation drops PI_ir
  threefold and distorts R from 0.63 to 1.34 while DC stays high.
- **Contact quality degrades substantially with time-on-sensor.** Over an hour of captures the pulsatile
  AC fell 3.4x with DC unchanged, and recovered fully after a rest. Any threshold comparison must control
  for this; pooling windows across sessions hides it.

## Known-incomplete
- **A per-frame LED current budget.** Implemented — `applyFrame()` now scales a frame down rather than
  letting it collapse the rail. Worth knowing what it does and does not protect: the boost leaves DCM at
  ~105 mA (~57 mA on a low battery), but `+5VL` does not reach the WS2812B 3.5 V floor until ~255 mA on a
  good battery and ~107 mA at the worst corner. So the budget guards the weak-battery corner; bright
  presets are not a general overload. An earlier revision of this file said otherwise.
- **FIFO overflow fires continuously and nobody knows why.** The overflow counter and the ALC overflow flag
  are now read and logged, which is new - nothing had ever read them. On hardware OVF_COUNTER is non-zero on
  virtually every service call: 4.8 events/s against a 5/s cadence, 25.5 lost samples/s against a 25 Hz
  production rate. Acting on it (the review's unqualified "hard invalidation") cleared the sample window
  every 200 ms and stopped the badge measuring anything at all, so it is deliberately observe-only.
  Candidate explanations, none yet distinguished: the part producing faster than the configured rate; the
  drain loop's `available = (wr - rd) & 0x1F` aliasing a full FIFO to empty and stalling; or the counter not
  clearing as the datasheet describes.

  **Probably not worth chasing.** Heart rate stays stable at the same BPM across every session while this
  fires, SpO2 works, and no user-visible symptom has ever been traced to it - whereas acting on it stopped
  the badge measuring at all. It is an unexplained counter, not a demonstrated defect, and it was
  previously described here as the subsystem's most concrete open question, which overstated it.
- **The vendor kernel reads one element past the end of its input buffer** (`an_x[100]` in a 100-element
  array). It is not a pure function of its input, and reported heart rate can shift with linker layout.
  Fixing it means forking the vendor DSP; the gating work contains the user-visible consequence instead.
